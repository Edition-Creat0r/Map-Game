# 0.3.3D economy foundation

Branch: `feature/0.3.3d-economy`, based on `feature/0.3.3c-terrain-polish` (53911ac). No automatic merge. Economy persistence and construction are **Singleplayer only**.

## Audit and architecture

The old `economy.js` already used `mapgame_economy_021e`, stored balances and placed-building IDs, capped offline money at 12 hours, and exposed purchase/subscription hooks. It only produced money; resource production, workforce and upkeep were absent. Spending and registration used separate writes, duplicate purchase IDs could charge twice, and registration discarded some placement fields. Its online and offline multipliers were both 1×. This version explicitly introduces the requested 1.2× visible-online production rule.

`buildings.js` already supplies useful catalog identities, footprints, variants and Regular/3D models. Those models remain. `shop.js` is a working Babylon preview component, while the old shop UI lives inside `game.js`. The starter shop does not mount that extra engine. `ui.js` supplies presentation styles, not an economy model. `game.js` saves world claims/capital under `mapGame_alpha021_firstPlayable` and preserves unrelated fields by merging. Basic 2D territory entry returned before the old 3D placement/restore path, so usable 2D construction needed an explicit connection.

The consolidated design is:

- `economy_model.js`: pure deterministic simulation, numeric validation, spending, and shared-terrain/footprint checks; also importable by Node tests.
- `economy.js`: the existing public API and save key, migration, exclusive browser writer lease, atomic purchase writes, lifecycle and UI notifications.
- `buildings.js`: five configurable starter rule sets attached to existing catalog IDs. Other catalog entries/models remain for legacy records; new purchases are limited to the starter set.
- `economy_ui.js`: categorized text shop and expandable world statistics, constructed lazily and refreshed only while visible.
- `basic_world.js`: free placement preview, rotation/cancellation, and simple world-coordinate building footprints drawn after cached terrain/forest imagery. Terrain imagery and forest candidates are not regenerated by construction.

The terrain, biome, forest, hydrology, water-renderer and strategic-map source files are unchanged from 0.3.3C. There is no per-pixel economy work. Basic and Regular remain separate; this update connects the currently active Basic renderer and retains the existing Regular models/preview.

## Economy rules

New worlds start with $400,000, 100 steel, 40 carbon, zero population and zero installed energy/housing. Existing iron/concrete/glass fields and balances are retained for compatibility but are not starter purchase requirements.

| Building / existing ID | Purchase | Existing footprint | Operation | Base output | Upkeep/min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Residential Home / `smallHouse` | $12,000 + 2 steel | 18×22 m | 1 energy | 20 housing; growth 2 people/min when powered | $1 |
| Small Power Plant / `powerPlant` | $25,000 + 10 steel + 5 carbon | 122×104 m | No workers or input power | 40 energy capacity | $6 |
| Steel Factory / `steelMill` | $45,000 + 15 steel + 5 carbon | 118×92 m | 10 energy, 6 workers | 6 steel/min | $12 |
| Carbon Facility / `carbonPlant` | $35,000 + 10 steel | 126×96 m | 8 energy, 4 workers | 4 carbon/min | $10 |
| Commercial Store / `cornerStore` | $18,000 + 4 steel | 24×20 m | 3 energy, 4 workers | $180/min | $8 |

Money/steel/carbon output is multiplied by 1.2 while Singleplayer is visible and advancing normally: e.g. 7.2 steel, 4.8 carbon, or $216/min. Energy capacity, housing, population growth and upkeep are not boosted. Energy is capacity rather than a stored battery resource. Housing exists immediately; residents grow into it. Available workforce is `floor(population × 0.6)`. Population never exceeds housing, and blackouts stop growth rather than deleting residents. Two starter homes eventually provide enough workers for all three starter businesses. Energy, population and workers are shared across the local world; no commuting/transport network is simulated.

Simulation uses fixed 10-second steps, checkpointed once per second outside the render loop. Purchases and other balance mutations first settle any partial interval with the **old** buildings/balances, so new construction never receives retroactive production. Partial elapsed and boosted time are persisted. Resume catches up at 1× for at most 12 hours (4,320 steps); excess elapsed time is discarded by advancing the saved checkpoint to now. A visible callback gap above 60 seconds is conservatively treated as offline rather than receiving the online bonus. Hidden/Home/Central time receives no online bonus. Repeated resume/reload at the same timestamp awards nothing twice. Backward clock changes do not move the checkpoint backward.

Generators receive operating priority, then buildings follow saved construction order. Each step reserves upkeep, workers and energy before activating a building. A shortage pauses the whole building with a specific reason. Paused buildings consume no upkeep, workers or energy. Income cannot finance upkeep within that same step. There are no negative balances/debt, loans, or automatic bailout grants. Resource balances are finite and capped at 10^12. Requested power/workers and deficits are shown separately from actual use.

Construction costs are paid only at successful placement. Opening, rotating, moving, cancelling with Escape/button, or leaving the territory spends nothing. Matching transaction IDs are idempotent; replaying an ID with a different type/site/rotation fails. A successful building and its debit are serialized together in one localStorage write, with in-memory rollback on write failure. Completed buildings have no demolition/refund UI in this phase.

## Placement and UI

The shop lists all five starters by category, with purchase materials, footprint, base/online output, upkeep, energy and workforce requirements. Purchases are disabled when unaffordable, outside a local territory, or outside Singleplayer. The Economy dock button expands/collapses statistics for Money, Steel, Carbon, Energy, Population, workforce and operating reasons. No shop preview engine or shop DOM rebuild runs while closed.

Construction uses `mapGameSharedTerrain.local`, including the authoritative water flag/buildability and normalized steepness. The footprint is sampled on a grid no coarser than 16 m, including its boundaries. Water/blocked samples and steepness above 0.54 prohibit starter construction. This is sampled terrain checking, not a continuous shoreline-polygon intersection. Decorative drainage overlays never participate. Footprints must fit within the 32,768-unit territory and must not overlap saved buildings (including rotated conservative bounds and a 2 m clearance). Existing capital sites reserve a 150×150 m area. No starter road/capital prerequisite was added, so the initial economy can bootstrap.

Positions remain local territory coordinates anchored to `T_x_y`; shared terrain sampling converts them to the same global world coordinates as before. Visual 512-unit chunk boundaries do not change placement. Buildings are simple roof/footprint markers with close-zoom labels, not a replacement building-art engine. Existing forest candidates stay unchanged; no resource clearing or forest editing is implemented.

## Save compatibility and concurrency

The economy retains `mapgame_economy_021e`. Balances and `placedIncomeItems` remain at their legacy locations; additive `economy.version = 1` stores population, checkpoint, partial timing, revision and migration metadata. Root/nested/placement unknown fields survive normalization. The world save remains separate and retains its unknown fields.

On first migration, canonical economy records take precedence; missing IDs from legacy world `buildings` are imported and deduplicated. Import happens once. Footprints are supplied from existing definitions where missing. Existing buildings are marked as legacy and retain their old money-output/housing/power definitions; populated legacy saves retain the old 18-power allowance. Initial legacy population is inferred from existing housing. New construction uses the new starter rules. Legacy buildings now use deterministic whole-building power allocation; the former proportional water/air economy is not simulated in the starter system. Unknown building types are retained but inactive. No records are silently replaced by new starters.

A newer economy version, malformed economy JSON, unavailable storage, or failed write pauses the economy and preserves the source record. `navigator.locks` provides an exclusive writer for this origin; another playing tab is read-only and must close the writer/reload to take over. Home tabs refresh incoming state without awarding progress. Acquiring a writer lease rereads the latest canonical record, and late matching storage events are ignored. An active writer encountering an unexpected external edit pauses rather than overwriting it. Lease release on pagehide allows reload; persisted-page return reacquires it. HTTPS or localhost and Web Locks support are required for writes.

Closing Home or playing Central no longer overwrites Singleplayer claims through the generic world-save hook. Supabase schema, auth, ownership and permissions are unchanged. There are no multiplayer economy writes or claims of multiplayer security. Local files/device clocks remain user-editable; there is no anti-cheat or cross-device/cloud economy synchronization. Before downgrading, back up both save keys and restore a matching pre-upgrade snapshot; old releases do not understand version-1 partial timing/migration flags.

## Validation and performance

38 Node tests pass, including the existing 23 terrain/biome/forest tests. Economy cases cover elapsed-time partitioning, online bonus and unboosted upkeep, offline cap/reload/backward clocks, partial construction boundaries, growth/workforce, energy shortages, insolvency, invalid costs/NaN, atomic/idempotent purchase, post-debit write failure rollback, cancellation, legacy/unknown fields, one-time migration, terrain/overlap/capital rules, writer conflicts, and late storage events.

Real browser checks cover all five starter purchases, free cancellation, keyboard rotation, growth/production/deficits/recovery, insufficient-funds buttons, real shared-water rejection, overlap, complete save/reload, legacy claims/unknown fields, Home closure, competing tabs, visible building restoration, local pan/zoom and return to World Map. Existing terrain and forest suites pass continuity, 120 stable roots, bounded caches (1,280 candidates/20 tiles in the tested forest), zero idle candidate builds, streaming reopen and visible rivers/lakes. Core navigation/auth-entry checks pass; authenticated multiplayer was not exercised. Final checked runs contain no console errors, failed requests or HTTP failures, including Babylon.js and Supabase loading. TLS verification remains enabled using the already approved environment trust store.

Chromium 154, 1440×1000, headless software WebGL. Three interleaved runs against exact 0.3.3C HTML/JS/CSS and this branch; medians below. These gesture results include machine scheduling and are not a production GPU benchmark.

| Measurement | 0.3.3C | 0.3.3D |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Engine ready | 1,749 ms | 1,840 ms |
| Territory entry | 799 ms | 787 ms |
| World Map pan FPS / p95 | 60.0 / 16.7 ms | 60.0 / 16.8 ms |
| Local pan FPS / p95 | 60.0 / 16.8 ms | 60.0 / 16.8 ms |
| Zoom FPS / p95 | 60.0 / 16.8 ms | 60.0 / 16.8 ms |
| High-detail pan FPS / p95 | 58.8 / 16.7 ms | 60.0 / 16.8 ms |
| At least 12 detail chunks, including wheel input | 2,534 ms | 2,569 ms |
| Long tasks >50 ms in each run | 2, 2, 2 | 2, 2, 2 |
| Maximum long task per run | 251, 200, 179 ms | 218, 189, 191 ms |

The approximately 91 ms startup increase and 35 ms detail-ready increase are retained in the results. No claim of a rendering speedup is based on the small FPS differences. A separate core regression recorded a 374 ms startup task, so startup spikes are not eliminated.

Dense-forest LOD stress is more variable than the representative gestures. A matched follow-up measured LOD transitions at 53.6 → 45.5 FPS (p95 33.4 ms in both) and maximum-zoom pan at 49.1 → 52.7 FPS (p95 33.4 ms in both). An earlier economy run measured 52.8/50.1 FPS for these phases. This remains a performance concern; stable 60 FPS at every forest/zoom combination is not established. Forest identities, geography, progressive caches and their limits remain intact. The normal shop-closed gesture medians do not conceal this stress result.

A separate final browser pan with six restored buildings measured 55.4 FPS, p95 33.3 ms (maximum 33.4 ms). A targeted dense-forest CPU trace attributed 8.36 ms to the economy/model/UI call stacks across 9.03 seconds of sampled time (including idle/native time). This supports keeping economy work out of the hot terrain loop; it does not explain all GPU/frame variability. Raw trace: `economy.cpuprofile`; aggregate: `cpu-summary.json`.

A six-building, full-12-hour pure-model catch-up completed all 4,320 steps in a median 4.65 ms over seven Node runs (range 4.31–24.60 ms); this excludes browser persistence/UI cost. Simulation and storage remain on the main thread, outside per-pixel rendering. Large-city catch-up and thousands of building markers have not been validated.

The final storage-event and shortcut fixes affect lifecycle/placement, not terrain/forest rendering. The final economy interaction run and targeted CPU profile run after those fixes; the interleaved table precedes those small fixes.
